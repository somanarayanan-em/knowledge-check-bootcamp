## Accuracy flags for the training team

- **Q16 (replaced).** The first draft tested the slide 21 takeaway "Build in dependency order (Database → Backend → API → Frontend → Security → Operations)". It was dropped for three reasons: the deck gives only that one line and no explanation (the notes say "Do not expand"), it does not say whether "build" means writing the design documents or building the code, and the order varies from team to team, so it is risky to assess. Q16 now tests handling a mid-design change to a technology decision, which is grounded in slides 12 and 14 notes. Training team: if you want slide 21 assessed, please clarify what "build" refers to. Option D is a deliberate trap that skips the impact check.
- **Q20 (security timing).** Slide 19 says "Design security early, not last". Slide 21 lists Security after Frontend in its build order. We follow slide 19 for Q20, as the training team advised: security is designed early and may be built later in implementation. Q20 is about when security is designed, not when it is built.
- **Q2.** The deck table maps data requirements to "Database schema, entity relationships". The example requirement ("Each allocation must record who approved it and when") is ours, loosely based on the slide 6 example (approver_id, approved_at).
- **Q3.** Uses the slide 6 requirement (FR3.1) in plain language, and lists the five design areas in non-technical words.
- **Q14 (wording).** The deck's note says that handing AI the full list and saying "do all of this" means "it will invent content", and slide 11 warns that without constraints AI may propose "technologies your organization does not use". The correct answer extends these two points: documents get filled with decisions nobody agreed, which may clash with the organization's requirements and policies. The link to organizational requirements and policies is our extension, not a deck quote. Please confirm the training team is comfortable with it.
- **Q10 (sequence).** Tests the order of steps in a greenfield project (constraints are settled after the summary is verified and before the structure is proposed), not recognition of the checklist. The checklist items now appear only in the Why. The deck does not number this exact sequence for greenfield. It is inferred from slide 8 (step order), slide 12 ("Only after constraints are set should you propose scaffolding") and slide 14. Option C is a deliberate trap: the knowledge base step in slide 10 applies only to brownfield. "Greenfield" is deliberately not defined in the stem. Q12 covers the greenfield and brownfield difference.
- **Q11.** The deck's example names specific tools. This question uses generic wording only, as agreed, so it tests the principle (pin versions, state exclusions, state the architecture approach) and not the example stack. The "one application rather than many small services" wording generalizes slide 13's "monolith, not microservices".
- **Q15.** The first draft was a near repeat of Phase 1 Q16 (split into clearly named files), so it now tests another document type (workflow and business rules design) in the same style as Q13. The option descriptions are paraphrased from the slide 17 subtitles.
- **Q13.** Tests one document type (frontend design). The descriptions in the options are paraphrased from the slide 16 and 17 subtitles. Other document types are available for swaps.
- **Q18.** Uses the "100 users" example from slide 19, with generic wording ("many separate services") in place of "microservices".
- **Q19.** Uses the "version 17 versus version 18" example from slide 19. The tool name is removed so non-technical readers are not distracted.
- **Q7 and Q8 (knowledge base).** Concept level only, as agreed. The deck defers the knowledge base to Day 2, and the notes say "Park the deep questions". These questions touch R4 conceptually (c in the matrix). They do not test setup steps. The AIDE skills link (slide 10 and slide 14) is not tested here. R3 is deferred.
- **Q7 (wording).** Slide 10 gives two consequences of skipping the knowledge base: AI cannot understand the patterns that already exist, and it cannot see the regression impact of project changes. The quiz centers the correct answer on the design being guided by an accurate picture of the whole existing system (technology, architecture, dependencies and patterns, per the slide 10 list). It does not reduce the knowledge base to patterns or to regression impact alone. Impact appears only in the Why. The option wording also keeps the team guiding the design, not AI acting on its own.
- **Q4 (wording).** The slide 19 notes say tagging gives "enough grain that a later defect can name which requirement was missed". The quiz deliberately uses a more neutral wording ("anything that cannot be traced can be questioned"), because a later defect does not always come from a missed requirement. It could come from a misread requirement, a build error or a wrong requirement.
- **Q6 (wording).** The deck says "Verify the summary" and does not say what to do when non-functional requirements are absent from it. The quiz treats two possible causes as equally likely: AI missed them in its summary, or the requirements are genuinely silent. The correct answer has AI re-read the requirements, check specifically for non-functional requirements, and flag a real gap if there is one. The "flag, do not silently expand" part is borrowed from the lab notes on slides 22 to 24. Please confirm this matches how the training team wants a gap handled.
- **R2 (not tagged).** R2 is the hands-on requirement, which is assessed by practical sign-off outside this knowledge check, so no question is tagged R2. The six questions that were first tagged R2 (Q4, Q5, Q10, Q11, Q12 and Q16) now carry R1 or R6, according to what each one tests. Q4, Q11 and Q12 are R1. Q5, Q10 and Q16 are R6.
- **Lab logistics.** Branch swapping and team assignments are not tested.
- **Crash Course versus Bootcamp.** No question depends on which lab variant a Champion attended.
